Digital Image Processing Foundations & Defense Q&A

Review these answers before your project viva to confidently answer questions on color channels, CLAHE, morphological operators, and clinical DR grading.

1 Why Green Channel instead of Grayscale?

In standard RGB fundus cameras, the red channel is over-saturated with choroidal back-scatter, while the blue channel suffers from severe Rayleigh scattering and low penetration. Oxygenated hemoglobin has its highest optical absorption around 540–570 nm (green band). Hence, retinal blood vessels and microaneurysms appear with maximum contrast in the green channel.

2 What is CLAHE and why not Global Histogram Equalization?

Standard global histogram equalization uses the whole image histogram, which over-amplifies noise in relatively homogeneous fundus backgrounds. CLAHE operates on small contextual tiles (8x8) and clips the histogram at a clip limit (2.5) before redistribution. This enhances subtle vessel edges and faint lesions while keeping background noise suppressed.

3 How does Morphological Blood Vessel Segmentation work?

We employ Morphological Top-Hat ($I - \text{Opening}(I)$) and Bottom-Hat transforms with structuring elements larger than the maximum vessel width. Morphological closing removes narrow vessel valleys to estimate the background illumination profile. Subtracting the enhanced image from background yields pure vessel signal, followed by Adaptive Gaussian Thresholding.

4 Why must the Optic Disc be masked before Exudate detection?

The optic nerve head (optic disc) is naturally the brightest, yellowish circular feature in the retina. Both hard exudates and the optic disc have high pixel intensities and yellow hues. Without finding and masking the optic disc circle, thresholding algorithms would wrongly classify the entire optic disc as a massive exudate lesion!

International Clinical Diabetic Retinopathy Disease Severity Scale (ETDRS)

Stage Disease Level Ophthalmoscopic Hallmarks (DIP Findings) Clinical Action Plan
Stage 0 No Apparent Retinopathy Zero microaneurysms, zero exudates, intact regular vasculature Annual dilated eye examination
Stage 1 Mild NPDR Microaneurysms only (small red capillary outpouchings) Rescreen in 6–12 months, optimize HbA1c
Stage 2 Moderate NPDR Multiple microaneurysms, hard lipid exudates, cotton-wool spots Referral to ophthalmologist within 2–4 weeks
Stage 3 Severe NPDR >20 intraretinal hemorrhages in 4 quadrants, venous beading, IRMA Urgent ophthalmologist review within 1–2 weeks
Stage 4 Proliferative DR (PDR) Neovascularization (new abnormal fragile vessels), vitreous hemorrhage Immediate specialist care (Anti-VEGF / Laser Photocoagulation)
